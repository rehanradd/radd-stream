**The defect, as reported by the owner at 18:05 against the live edge on the real
150 799-post catalog:** `GET /api/v1/search/by-media?q=` — `inception` 318 ms,
`kpop` 580 ms, `don` 7 742 ms, `love` 44 992 ms, `a` **never returned**
(60 071 ms client timeout). Not a caching problem and not an answer-size problem:
"it is the *work done to produce* the answer". `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on the old
statement said `SCAN p` + `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR GROUP BY` +
`USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY`, because one SQL text joined the 1 780 121-row
`releases` and the 4 073 865-row `download_sources` under every matched post,
grouped, and sorted every group to display ten rows. `LIMIT` bounded the response,
never the work. Behind it, nginx `proxy_read_timeout 30s` turned the slow queries
into 504s and the never-returned one into a spinner.

**Landed (`ab83f1c` + `b2d5d9b`, branch `q3-search-latency`).** In
`Fast_Fetch/media_search.py`:
- Candidates come from ONE newest-first scan of `posts` (`ORDER BY p.id DESC
  LIMIT ?`, `CANDIDATE_WINDOW = 200`) that carries the projection columns itself.
  An earlier three-arm shape (exact / prefix / contains as separate bounded
  queries) is in this history and was **rejected**: it tripled the rowid walk and
  measured 2.8x slower than the replaced statement for a whole-title query.
- Relevance tier (2 exact / 1 prefix / 0 contains) is computed in Python over the
  capped window and is the **first** sort key, ahead of print-and-mirror count. The
  old `ORDER BY (release_count + source_count) DESC` survives, over the window.
- `q` shorter than `MIN_QUERY_CHARS = 2` issues **no SQL at all** and answers empty
  with a `hint`. A 1-character token was never a query; the old code treated it as
  one and paid 150 799 joins for it.
- `%` and `_` are escaped, so two keystrokes cannot build a wildcard cliff.
- `redact.adult_clause('p')` sits in the WHERE of the only scan, so the bail cannot
  be sorted around. Counts are read back through `idx_releases_post`/`idx_sources_rel`
  for the capped ids; evidence comes from `cat_work_sites.post_id`, never
  `canonical_key`.
- Recall boundary recorded in the docstring as a fact, not hidden: candidates are
  the *newest* 200 matches, so an old-but-popular title can be missed by a broad
  query that the old whole-catalog popularity sort would have surfaced. That is the
  price of bounding the work before the join, and it is the one behavioural
  regression the owner should know about.

**Two findings outside the brief, reported not fixed.**
1. `/api/v1/posts?q=` — the keystroke route the Android app actually calls — still
   carries a cliff: **66 322 ms** for `inception`. **Corrected on two counts**: that was
   an *in-process* query against my copy (not a live-edge timing; I took no edge sample,
   because the route holds a service thread), and its cause is **not** a missing index.
   `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on production for the two statements `viewer.py:2633-2652` builds
   reads `SCAN p` for the exact-total `COUNT(*)` **and** `SCAN p` for the page, with both
   joins already covered by indexes and **no** `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY` at the
   default sort: the cost is two full scans with three leading-wildcard `LIKE`s, the one
   predicate a B-tree cannot serve. The fix is `MIN_QUERY_CHARS=2`, `_escape_like`
   (`viewer.py:2546` interpolates `%`/`_` unescaped, so `q=%` is every row), and dropping
   the exact `total` in favour of `has_more` for `q=` requests. Also answers `q=a` with 10
   rows instead of refusing the non-query.
2. **WRONG, and the error is the lesson.** I wrote "`cat_work_sites.post_id` has no
   index at all". It does: `idx_cat_work_sites_post`, on
   `/home/ubuntu/radd-stream-data/merged_catalog_20261025.db` (150 799 posts, 275 488
   `cat_work_sites` rows), verified from `sqlite_master`. `releases.post_id` and
   `download_sources.release_id` are indexed too (`idx_releases_post`, `idx_sources_rel`),
   and `posts` carries `idx_posts_canonical_key`/`_imdb`/`_slug`/`_tmdb`/`_source_ind`.
   Cause: `catalog_v2.ensure_schema()` creates neither `releases` nor `download_sources`
   and no join indexes, so a schema-built file plans `SCAN releases` where production
   plans a covering-index search — and a number off it is that file's number. Now pinned
   by `test_posts_query_plan.py`.
